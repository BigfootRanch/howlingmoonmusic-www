The Story

They called her scarlet. Called him the devil. Built a fire from lies and waited for both of them to burn.

They didn’t.

Ghost Wanted Dead or Alive begins inside that Southwest-gothic world—preachers, sinners, whispered accusations and two people who found something holy in the very love everyone else condemned. But this album doesn’t stay inside one story. It rides into the real battles that make outlaws of ordinary people: losing a child, fighting a bottle, surviving judgment, growing older, defending a love and finding the one pair of brown eyes that can bring you home.

Fifteen songs about people who were buried by somebody else’s version of the truth—and kept breathing anyway.

01
LIAR, PREACHER MAN
“Saturday Drunk. Sunday Saved.”

He raised a Bible on Sunday while another woman’s perfume still clung to him from Saturday night.

I protected his reputation, raised his children and denied every whisper—until the day he turned his own sins into my scarlet letter. This isn’t an attack on faith. It is an exposure of the kind of man who learns that sounding holy can hide almost anything.

Truth doesn’t need a preacher. It only needs one witness who finally stops protecting the lie.

02
I DIDN'T DIE
“They Wanted Ashes. She Walked Out Alive.”

They painted me scarlet with a preacher’s brush, called me fallen and invited everyone I loved to stand around the fire.

They fed it with twisted scripture, blocked doors, sharpened gossip and prayers disguised as punishment. They expected shame to finish what their lies started.

But truth doesn’t burn. When the smoke cleared, I was still standing—and the scarlet letter they gave me had become proof that I survived them.

03
ONE NIGHT WITH THE DEVIL
“The Sinner Was the Only One Who Showed Her Mercy.”

They called him the devil before I ever knew his heart.

He wasn’t my plan. He wasn’t my type. But when the righteous people were throwing stones, the blamed-up sinner with the steady hand was catching my tears. They called our love the fall of a good woman.

I called it the first night somebody loved me without asking me to apologize for being alive.

04
SMOKE ME
“After the Fire, They Finally Learned How to Breathe.”

We left the small-town whisper game behind until the headlights faded and the world disappeared.

Inside a cabin far from the people who judged us, there were no pews, no accusations and no one demanding a confession. Just two scarred people discovering that touch could be tender, desire could be sacred and love could grow in everything meant to destroy us.

They gave us smoke. We made it holy.

05
RODEO COWBOY
“The Hardest Ride of His Life Was Coming Home.”

JT was a bull rider. Eight seconds on the back of something determined to throw you—and enough adrenaline to make the demons go quiet.

But the most dangerous bull wasn’t in the arena. It was the pain, anger and alcohol inside him that nearly destroyed the only love that ever gave him a reason to come home. I didn’t sign up to ride that chaos, but loving him meant fighting to survive it too.

The arena was our marriage. The bull was everything trying to break us. When the buzzer sounded, somehow, we were both still standing.

06
GHOST WANTED DEAD OR ALIVE
“They Buried Him in Lies. Then Watched Him Thrive.”

They watched him, tracked him and built every rumor bigger than the one before it. He was hiding. He was finished. He was dead. He was somewhere in Mexico.

There was only one problem: he never fell.

The monster they created walked out of their story, built a life and refused to explain himself. Now they cannot stop chasing the ghost of the man they failed to destroy.

He didn’t disappear. He simply became unreachable—and thriving was the revenge they never prepared for.

07
HELD YOU FIRST
“They Told His Child Someone Else Was Daddy.”

This is a father’s song.

After betrayal, flashing lights and one slammed door, his child was pulled crying from his arms. He missed birthdays and first days while somebody else tried to erase him from his own child’s life.

He could let heartbreak bury him—or live long enough for that child to someday find the truth. So he put down the bottle, faced himself and built a better man from the wreckage.

They may have stolen the years. They can never change who held that baby first.

08
DROWN IN THE BOTTLE E
“The Bottle Promised Relief. It Was Digging a Grave.”

This isn’t a drinking song. It is the moment someone finally recognizes that the thing numbing the pain is also swallowing their life.

Sobriety isn’t a pretty sunrise montage. It is shaking hands, sleepless ceilings, shame burning off slowly and feeling everything the alcohol helped you escape.

The victory begins with one terrifying sentence: I won’t die in this bottle. Tonight, I choose life.

09
HE JUST CALLED MY NAME
“Everybody Believes in Miracles—Until God Gives One to You.”

People love to sing about grace, redemption and second chances. But let God bless someone whose past they have already decided should disqualify them—and suddenly they become the judge.

This song isn’t angry at God. It calls out the spiritual hypocrisy of people who praise mercy on Sunday and ration it on Monday. They wanted my history to be my life sentence.

Then He called my name—and their permission was never required.

10
WISH YOU WERE ME
“Maybe You Don’t Hate Me. Maybe You Hate That I Survived.”

Some people study your life harder than they live their own.

They watch, compare, criticize and turn every blessing into another reason to resent you. But hatred that obsessive usually has something hiding underneath it: they aren’t furious because you failed. They’re furious because everything meant to break you didn’t.

You don’t want my scars or the price I paid to get here. You just wish the fire had given you what it gave me.

11
LOST IN SANTA FE
“Trouble Couldn’t Find Us There.”

We didn’t get lost in Santa Fe. We disappeared into it.

Turquoise dreams, adobe moonlight, tribal drums, wood flutes, coyotes and desert sky—while every weight we carried vanished beneath the stars. With my bull-riding cowboy beside me, the whole world finally became quiet.

Sometimes getting lost isn’t confusion. It is finding the one place where fear no longer knows your name.

12
BROWN EYES SAY
“One Look Stopped Me From Burning Everything Down.”

Bad day. Bills on the table. Blood boiling. One more match and I’m ready to burn every bridge between me and the rest of the world.

Then those brown eyes look at me.

No lecture. No fixing. No fighting my fire with more fire. Just the dog who knows exactly how to pull me back before I lose myself.

Sometimes the one who saves you never says a word.

13
ME & MY DOG | SPURS
“The Cowboy Got Old. His Dog Got Old Beside Him.”

The spurs are hanging on the wall now.

The hands that once turned wrenches and curled into fists rest beside a Bible, an old guitar and the dog who rode shotgun through every wild year. There’s gray in one muzzle and gray on one chin. Both have slowed down, and neither needs the roar anymore.

After all the rodeos, bar fights and busted ribs, peace sounds like a tail keeping time beside a rocking chair.

14
WORLD MELTS AWAY
“They Said We Were More Than Friends. They Were Right.”

They whispered about us in corners while we played it cool. Then one sunset, you took my hand—and we stopped living for everybody else.

From the Smokies to the Gulf, through desert skies and moonlit shorelines, you became my shelter, my escape, my wild and my stay. When I’m with you, trouble loses our address.

The world doesn’t become perfect. It simply disappears.

15
LEFT MY HEART IN SANTA FE
“Some Cities Give You Memories. Santa Fe Keeps Part of Your Soul.”

Adobe walls still hold our names. Your laughter lives in the canyon stone. Every turquoise trail, coyote cry and painted sunset leads me back to the love we left burning beneath that desert sky.

This isn’t just a song about missing a city. It is about a place becoming inseparable from the person you loved there.

I came home from Santa Fe. My heart never did.
