The Story

Not all grief comes with a funeral.

Sometimes the person you miss is still alive but no longer speaks to you. Sometimes the last ordinary moment becomes a goodbye without warning. Sometimes a birthday arrives for someone you can’t call. Sometimes you sit beside a hospital bed and watch the future you planned disappear one heartbeat at a time.

These eleven songs are for those nights.

The nights when the phone stays silent, the coffee turns bitter and being strong becomes one more performance you cannot survive. This album doesn’t tell you to wipe your face, count your blessings or move on.

Tonight, the tears win.

Let them.

The Songs

01
TEARDROPS IN MY COFFEE
“The Cruelest Grief Is Missing Someone Who Is Still Alive.”

The kitchen is quiet. The clock keeps ticking. I reach for the sugar, pour in forgiveness and stir another memory into the cup.

Some heartbreak doesn’t explode. It returns every ordinary morning when you check a phone that still hasn’t moved. She is somewhere in the world, breathing and living, while I sit here loving someone I cannot reach.

Sweet turns bitter as I stir—but I still pray that love eventually finds its way home.

02
HAZY BUBBLE
“I Wasn’t at Peace. I Was in Shock.”

Grief doesn’t always look like crying. Sometimes it feels like floating outside your own life.

People speak and you nod, but you never hear them. The world spins double. Silence sits on your chest. Every step is heavy, everything is empty and you cannot remember whether you ate—or who you were before they left.

Maybe I’ll breathe again someday. Right now, I’m trapped inside the hazy bubble grief built around me.

03
COFFEE CONSTELLATION
“When Did We Get So Good at Throwing People Away?”

One wrong word. One painful night. One text that lands wrong.

Block. Delete. Ghost. Done.

At a diner, coffee spills across my jeans and the stain looks like a crooked constellation—a little universe inside the mess. Maybe that is what love is too: imperfect, inconvenient and still worth cleaning up.

I don’t want to win if winning means losing you. Before we slam the door forever, leave a little room for us to be human.

04
ROCK A BYE BABY
“The Monster I Promised Wasn’t Real Is Lying Beside You.”

I was the mother who checked beneath the bed, turned on every light and promised my little girl that no monster could ever reach her.

Now I’m standing outside her life, watching her freeze when the phone lights up. Her words become careful. Her shoulders tighten. Her voice sounds like speaking freely might cost too much.

My silence is not surrender. It is the thousand cuts I will take to keep from making her life more dangerous. If this song ever finds her through the noise, I need her to remember: Mama is still here. There is always a way back home.

05
ALMOST CALLED
“I Didn’t Almost Call Because I Was Weak. I Almost Called Because I Still Chose You.”

We both said things. Saw red. Hit delete, block and erase—then smiled for the camera while everything inside us caved in.

I almost called when I saw your picture. When something funny happened. When I had good news, bad news or nothing important at all—because you were still the first person I wanted to tell.

Ego wants to win. Love wants to turn around.

I cannot change the past, but I can still build a bridge home. Give love a chance.

06
THE LAST TIME
“Nobody Tells You While the Last Time Is Happening.”

The last photograph. The last drive. The last kiss on the cheek before someone walks away like they’ll do it a thousand more times.

There are no flashing lights or warnings. Forever can disappear inside an ordinary day—and only later do you understand what that moment became.

If I had known, I would have held on longer, listened harder and memorized everything.

I thought we had more time. That is the part that breaks me.

07
HAPPY BIRTHDAY
“The Calendar Remembered Even When You Stopped Answering.”

I woke up already knowing the date. Your name was in my mind before my eyes were fully open.

I almost reached out like I always did—then remembered you would not reply. So I closed my eyes, pictured the candles and sang to the child I can no longer see. Every first laugh, first step and birthday before this one still lives inside me.

You may never hear it.

I said it anyway.

Happy birthday. Merry Christmas. I miss you. I still love you.

08
DROWNING IN MY TEARS
“I Was Drowning in Front of You. You Didn’t Even Flinch.”

This is not pretty crying. This is shaking, gasping and begging someone to care while they treat your breaking like background noise.

I’m unraveling in front of the person who was supposed to love me most—and the echo of their silence is colder than being alone. They built a cage from my love, sanctified the silence and called abandoning me a choice.

I am bare. Defeated. Exhausted.

Someone hear me.

Please.

I’m drowning.

09
NOT ENOUGH BOTTLES
“God Promised to Keep Every Tear. I Worried Heaven’s Bottle Wasn’t Big Enough.”

Scripture says God collects our tears in a bottle. He sees every one. Catches every one. Counts every one.

But some seasons produce more tears than bottles, buckets or oceans could hold. The nights grow long, mercy feels far and faith is the only thread still holding you together.

Then comes the possibility that changes the song: perhaps every tear was also a seed. Perhaps heaven has been watering something you cannot see yet.

If God counted all this sorrow, maybe joy is what comes next.

10
BROKEN HEART AND BUSTED DREAMS
“One Hospital Room Can Destroy an Entire Future.”

I’m sleeping in a chair with his hand inside mine, watching numbers on a screen and begging him not to leave—as though he gets a choice.

We were supposed to have forever. His coffee cup, his side of the bed, the ordinary mornings, the arguments, the vacations and growing old together. Suddenly, every dream we assumed we still had time to live is hanging from one more breath and one more heartbeat.

Don’t tell me time will heal this. Sit beside me in this hell and let me feel what I feel.

Tonight, I am still holding his hand.

11
IF TEARS COULD CALL HEAVEN
“I Would Give Every Tomorrow for One Yesterday With You.”

Your number is still in my phone. Your chair still belongs to you. I scroll through old posts just to stand inside the same air for a moment.

I don’t need a grand conversation. I miss hearing you talk about nothing. I miss the voice that could calm every storm with one small call.

If tears could reach heaven, I would never hang up. I would say “I love you” until it finally felt like enough.

But one more call would never be enough.
